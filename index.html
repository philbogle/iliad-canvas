How to use this canvas

This is an interactive study application for learning and reciting the opening lines of Homer's Iliad. Use the arrow buttons (or your keyboard's left/right arrows) to step through the text line-by-line. Click the Play button on the video to hear the correct reconstructed ancient pronunciation and pitch accents. You can click any word in the parsing grid to view a deep-dive popup containing its dictionary lemma, grammatical parsing, and Proto-Indo-European etymology.

Note: I am just beginning my journey learning Ancient Greek and rely on a variety of resources to build this canvas. Bug reports and corrections are welcome.

2. Classical Attic Pronunciation (5th c. BC)

  • W. Sidney Allen, Vox Graeca: The Pronunciation of Classical Greek (3rd ed., Cambridge University Press, 1987) — Standard authority on Classical Attic aspirated plosives [tʰ, pʰ, kʰ], monophthongs [eː, uː], front-rounded [y], and pitch accents.
  • A. M. Devine & Laurence D. Stephens, The Prosody of Greek Speech (Oxford University Press, 1994) — Empirical treatise on ancient Greek tonal pitch contours.

3. Metrical Scansion & Critical Text Editions

  • D. B. Monro & T. W. Allen (eds.), Homeri Opera, Tomus I: Ilias I–XII (Oxford Classical Texts / Clarendon Press).
  • Martin L. West, Greek Metre (Oxford University Press, 1982) & Homeri Ilias (Bibliotheca Teubneriana, 1998) — Canonical scholarship on dactylic hexameter, caesurae, synizesis, and correption.
  • Corinne Ondine Pache (ed.), The Cambridge Guide to Homer (Cambridge University Press, 2020) — Chapter on Homeric Meter.
  • Geoffrey Steadman, Homer's Iliad 1: Greek Text with Facing Vocabulary and Commentary (2013).
  • Perseus Digital Library (Tufts University, ed. Gregory Crane).
  • Perseids Project — The Morpheus API for automated morphological parsing.
  • Hypotactic (hypotactic.com) — Scansion data (dactylic hexameter feet and syllable lengths) was extracted from the Hypotactic Iliad CSV datasets.
